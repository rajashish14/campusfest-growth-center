Why Bengaluru

  • More colleges than any other district in India: 1,106 in Bengaluru Urban vs 703 in Jaipur and 491 in Hyderabad (AISHE 2021-22). sourced
  • Our three cheapest channels in 2025 (college clubs, referrals, ambassadors) run on campus density, not ad money. More campuses within an hour's commute means more groups we can sign.
  • Dense consumer-tech and D2C brand base for co-funded activations. 3 sponsor activations were already linked to attendee campaigns in 2025. dataset

Capacity check. In 2025, 69.8% of visitors came on day 1. Stretch means ~2,290 on day 1 against an assumed 2,600 safe daily limit A. Past ~3,700 unique visitors the limit is the venue, not marketing. In 2025 the 6 pm gate hour took 400 admissions, 2× a normal hour.

Why you can believe the number

  • Every rate is a 2025 rate for the same channel, taken from the cleaned dataset (26 duplicate rows removed, 60 staff, test and internal people excluded). Only the budget mix changes.
  • Diminishing returns are built in. Doubling a channel's budget gives 1.7× the people, not 2×.
  • Operational fixes are sized below the outside evidence. A 26-study meta-analysis found reminders cut no-shows by 25%. The base case assumes 15%.
  • The plan is reallocation, not new money. ₹1L comes out of Paid and Creators (₹274–337 per visitor) and goes into channels at ₹69–108.

What has to be true for the stretch

  • Each registrant brings 0.12 extra registrations through the squad referral.
  • One in five form-abandoners is recovered.
  • Group passes add 3 points to registration rate.
  • Reminders cut no-shows 25%.

What we deliberately will not do

  • Scale creators. ₹337 per visitor and 60% show. Creators bring curiosity, not commitment. Reduced to a ₹12k test of 2 micro-creators.
  • Book a celebrity headliner. It would take the whole budget and pull non-student crowds into a venue already capped. Mood Indigo cancelled its 2025 pre-registrations after crowd chaos at the gate.
  • Freebies for signing up. They inflate low-intent registrations, and free events already see a ~28% median no-show.
  • Hoardings and broad awareness. No way to attribute them to people.

Dependencies and overlap

  • Bet 3 lifts every other channel, so it goes live first.
  • Retargeting (bet 4) needs the traffic from bets 1–2.
  • Clubs and referrals reach the same students. First-touch person_id attribution prevents double counting.
  • Paid cuts happen in weeks 1–3 so the money can fund bets 1–3.

Backtest: these rules on the 2025 data

Replaying 2025 day by day in the Growth Center, the fatigue rule (CTR down ≥30% vs the previous 14 days) flags Paid Social on 16 March (2.7% → 1.8%). After that date Paid spent ₹25,800 for 61 visitors (₹423 each). At College Partnerships' ₹81 per visitor, the same money buys ~5× the visitors.

The same replay on 20 March projects ~1,720 visitors against the 2,200 goal (the actual was 2,050). The gap was visible three weeks before the event.

Leading indicators we already trust

  • Brochure download: confirmed people who downloaded it showed up 82.4% of the time vs 70.6% for those who did not (+12 pts). It is our earliest show-up signal.
  • Submitting an event question → 85.7% showed up.
  • Registrations vs the goal-pace line every week; projected visitors from the 14-day run-rate.
  • Cadence: a 5-minute daily check; a Monday review where at most 20% of the remaining budget moves per week.

How the Growth Center and the AI copilot drive these calls

  • Time machine: any date as it looked then, no future data leaking in.
  • Rule verdicts per campaign (Scale, Hold, Fix, Cut) and live initiative thresholds (Scale, Change, Stop) from this slide, editable.
  • Diagnosis feed → task in one click, with owner, deadline and the evidence attached.
  • Copilot: answers from live data via tool calls (trace shown), simulates budget shifts, says when the data can't answer, and drafts tasks or pauses that run only after the user confirms.
